Smart Signal

AI Traffic Lights for Emergency Vehicles

Team Ganga House · Delhi Public School Nerul · Class 5

The problem

Ambulances and fire trucks often get stuck at red lights, and every minute lost can matter.

Our question: can a traffic signal see an emergency vehicle coming and turn green by itself?

Our idea

A camera above the road looks for an ambulance or a fire truck. When the AI sees one, the siren sounds and the ambulance's side of the road gets a green corridor while all the other signals turn red. When the camera stops seeing an emergency vehicle for about 2 seconds, traffic returns to normal.

How it works

Lights + siren

The camera takes pictures of the yellow AI zone. The AI on the laptop checks whether they show an ambulance or a fire truck. If so, the laptop sends a message through a USB cable to the Arduino, and the Arduino switches the lights and the siren.

The camera checks about three pictures every second. When the ambulance has left the zone, the AI stops seeing an emergency vehicle. After 6 pictures in a row without one (about 2 seconds), the siren stops, the green light turns yellow for 2 seconds and the signals go back to normal.

Normal traffic has two modes (we choose with keys on the laptop):

  • Normal cycle: Hospital Road green for 10 seconds, yellow for 2 seconds, then Station Road green for 10 seconds, yellow for 2 seconds, and repeat.
  • Exhibition mode: Station Road stays green and Hospital Road stays red, so visitors can watch the ambulance change the lights straight away.

How we built it

  • We built a model city junction with two roads: Hospital Road and Station Road.
  • We wired the lights and tested each one.
  • We trained the AI in PictoBlox and tested it with toy vehicles.
  • The program is made of blocks. A variable called mode is 0 for normal traffic and 1 for an emergency.
  • A variable called phase remembers which light is on, so the program knows which signal must turn yellow first.
  • Two counters, emCount and clearCount, count pictures in a row with and without an emergency vehicle.
  • We made our own blocks, like "A green", "B red" and "Ambulance go", so the main program is short and easy to read.
✎
AI help, honestlyOur team is still learning to understand block coding, so we used Claude, an AI assistant, to help plan and check the program. We tested every light and every detection ourselves. When bugs came up, we asked the AI to fix them. For example, the lights were being switched on too rapidly, so we added a wait.

The electronics

Each light glows only when electricity flows in a complete circuit: from the Arduino pin, through the LED, and back through the ground wire. In the buzzer, a thin plate vibrates very fast and makes sound.

Buzzer (siren) on pin A0. The analog pins A0 and A1 can also work as ordinary on/off (digital) pins. We used A1 for one red light because the board was crowded and there was more space there.

Parts: Arduino Uno R4, four traffic light modules, one buzzer module, breadboard, jumper wires, USB webcam, laptop with PictoBlox.

Do real cities do this?

Yes. The idea is called Emergency Vehicle Preemption. Many cities use GPS, infrared or radio signals from the ambulance, and some use AI cameras. We tried the camera and AI method to learn how it works.

What next?

  • Train with more photos and different vehicles.
  • Make it work at night.
  • Put a camera on every road, so an ambulance from any direction gets a green light.
  • Also check that the siren is on, so only ambulances on an emergency call get a green light.
  • Connect many junctions into one green corridor all the way to the hospital.

FAQ

What is AI?

Computers doing things that normally need human thinking, like recognising pictures.

What is machine learning?

A branch of AI in which the computer learns from examples, instead of being told every rule.

What is the difference between AI and an ordinary program?

An ordinary program follows rules a person wrote, like "if the pin is HIGH, turn on the light". An AI program learns its rules from examples, like our photos of ambulances.

How does the computer "see"?

A picture is made of tiny dots called pixels. The computer finds patterns in them, like shapes and colours.

How did you train it?

We took about 60 photos of each of four classes and let the computer study them many times. Then we tested with toys in new places.

What is confidence?

How sure the AI is. We act only when it is more than 85% sure, in two pictures in a row.

How does it know the ambulance has passed?

When the ambulance leaves the AI zone, the AI stops seeing an emergency vehicle. After 6 pictures in a row without one (about 2 seconds), the siren stops, the green turns yellow for 2 seconds and the signals go back to normal.

What if a second ambulance comes?

If it reaches the AI zone while the light is still green, the green stays on, because the counter of "no ambulance" pictures starts again from zero. If it comes just after the first one has gone, it waits about 2 seconds for the yellow light to finish, then gets a green corridor again.

How long does the ambulance get a green light?

As long as the AI can see it in the zone, plus about 2 seconds after it leaves. There is no fixed time.

What if a car hides the ambulance?

We tested with cars around the ambulance, and the AI could still spot it. But if the ambulance is completely hidden, the camera cannot see it, just like our eyes. That is why the camera is up high, looking down.

What if an ambulance and a fire truck come together?

Both are emergency vehicles, so the AI treats them the same. The green corridor stays on until the zone is clear of both.

What if the camera stops working?

Then the AI sees no ambulance, so the lights carry on normally. They never get stuck on green. We can still use the keys on the laptop to start an emergency by hand.

What is the yellow box in the middle of the junction?

It is a box junction. Cars must not stop inside it, so the junction never gets blocked and an ambulance can always get through.

What if the AI makes a mistake?

That is why we use confidence. If the AI is not sure enough, nothing changes. We can also use keys on the laptop: M turns off the camera control, E starts an emergency, C clears it, and A gives control back to the camera.

What if the ambulance is not on an emergency call?

Our model cannot tell. In real life, sensors and the siren would help the system know that the ambulance is on an emergency call.

Does it work in the dark?

No. In the dark it got confused, so we added a light. Real systems would need night cameras.

Would it work on real ambulances?

Not yet. We trained it on toys. For real roads it would need thousands of real photos.

What does the Arduino do?

It is a small computer. It gets commands from the laptop and switches the lights and the siren through its pins.

Why is one red light on pin A1?

Analog pins can also work as ordinary on/off pins. The board was crowded, and A1 had more space for the wire.

How does an LED light up?

Electricity flows round a complete circuit: pin, LED, then the ground wire back.

How does the buzzer work?

A thin plate inside vibrates very fast and makes sound waves.

Why four signals?

There is one on each corner. In an emergency, only the signal facing the ambulance turns green. The other three turn red, including the traffic coming the other way on Hospital Road.

What if an ambulance comes on Station Road?

Our model has one camera, on Hospital Road. A real junction would need a camera on every road.

What if a second ambulance comes on another road at the same time?

Our model cannot see it, because our only camera is on Hospital Road. That ambulance would have to wait for its normal turn of green. A real junction would need a camera on every road and a rule for who goes first, for example "first to arrive goes first". The second ambulance would then get its own green corridor straight after, with yellow in between so both stay safe.

Why a camera and not GPS or infrared?

GPS cannot tell positions on a small model. The camera works with any vehicle and let us learn machine learning.

Did you write the code yourself?

We are still learning block coding, so we used Claude, an AI assistant, to help. We built and tested the model ourselves, and we can explain how the program works.

What is a variable? A loop?

A variable is a box that stores a value, like mode. A loop repeats blocks again and again.

What would you improve?

More photos, night use, a camera on every road, checking the siren, and many junctions joined into a green corridor.
